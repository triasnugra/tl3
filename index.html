HR Topic 1
Supervisor Responsibilities
Know your role deeply — KPI monitoring, agent retention, coaching, escalation, daily target push.
📊KPIs You Must Know Cold
KPIWhat It MeasuresYour Action as Supervisor
Call VolumeNumber of calls per agent per daySet daily targets. Check hourly via dashboard. If an agent is behind by 10AM, intervene immediately — not at EOD.
Conversion Rate% of calls resulting in a closed saleWeekly call analysis — find which stage agents are losing prospects. Script coaching per individual failure point.
Revenue / GMVTotal sales value by teamBreak down daily target per agent. Use visible leaderboard to drive healthy competition.
AHTAverage Handle Time per callMonitor outliers. Too long = struggling with objections. Too short = rushing. Coach both directions.
Retention Rate% of agents staying over timeMonthly career 1-on-1s. Spot burnout signals early — act before they resign.
🔺Escalation Framework
1
Agent signals escalation
Don't let them struggle silently. Intervene the moment they flag it.
2
Supervisor takes the call
Lead with empathy first — "I understand your frustration." De-escalate tone before addressing the issue.
3
Resolve or escalate to L2
Within authority → resolve with gesture if needed. Beyond authority → warm transfer with full context, no cold handoffs.
4
Post-call debrief with agent
Every escalation is a coaching moment. "Here's what we learned. Here's how to handle it next time."
🔄Daily Target Push — Your Routine
  • 8AM: Morning huddle — today's target, top focus, one quick tip.
  • 10AM: First check — who's on track, who needs a nudge.
  • 12PM: Midday pulse — re-share leaderboard. Create urgency for the second half.
  • 2PM: Quick coaching for anyone behind. One specific fix only — not a lecture.
  • 4PM: Final push — call out who's close to their target. Peer motivation kicks in.
  • EOD: Recap — celebrate wins publicly, log blockers for tomorrow's plan.
💡Strategic frame: Don't just push. Create visibility. When agents see the leaderboard in real time, they push themselves.
🤝Agent Retention — Proactive, Not Reactive
  • Monthly career 1-on-1s — focused on growth and challenges, not just numbers.
  • Early burnout detection: Drop in call quality, shorter calls, less engagement — act before they resign.
  • Stretch roles for top performers: Let them mentor a new agent or lead a mini-session. Boredom causes exits.
  • Visible recognition: Announce wins in team chat. People leave managers who don't see their effort.
  • Address grievances fast: Even if you can't fix it immediately, acknowledge it quickly.
  • Build team identity: Shared goals, team name, culture — people stay where they feel they belong.

HR Topic 2
Telesales Mastery
Know the full sales funnel — and explain how you improve each stage as a Supervisor.
Stage 1
📞 Opening
Hook in 10 seconds. Don't pitch yet — earn their attention first.
Stage 2
🔍 Discovery
Ask 2+ questions. Understand the need before you pitch.
Stage 3
🎯 Pitching
Present as a solution, not a feature list. Lead with "This means for you..."
Stage 4
🛡 Objection
Acknowledge → validate → reframe. Never argue. Solve the concern.
Stage 5
✅ Closing
Assumptive close. Create urgency. Ask for the decision confidently.
Stage 6
🔁 Follow-up
Not closed? Schedule next contact. Every "no" is a "not yet."
📈How You Improve Each Stage as a Supervisor
StageCommon Agent FailureYour Supervisor Action
OpeningGeneric, robotic — customer hangs up earlyA/B test 3 opening lines weekly. Track early hang-up rate. Roll out the lowest-drop version team-wide.
DiscoverySkips to pitch — agent doesn't listenCall review exercise: "Count how many questions the agent asks before pitching." Less than 2 = immediate coaching.
PitchingFeature dumping, no benefit framingScript rewrite: every feature followed by "This means for you…" — drill until it's natural, not read.
ObjectionAgent gets defensive or gives upBuild an Objection Playbook — top 5 objections with scripted responses. Weekly roleplay drill.
Closing"Do you want it?" — too weak, too passiveTeach assumptive close: "Should I go ahead and process this for you today?" Coach until the ask is confident.
📉Diagnosing Low Conversion
1
Pull data by stage
Where in the call do most drops happen? Opening, pitch, or objection? The data tells you where to focus.
2
Listen to 10–15 recordings personally
Numbers show what, recordings show why. You'll catch tone, pacing, and language the dashboard won't.
3
Map each agent's failure point
Agent A loses at objection. Agent B loses at close. Different problems = different coaching.
4
Fix one stage at a time
"This week, we're fixing one thing: the price objection." Focused beats scattered every time.
🎙️What a Great Call Looks Like — Know This Cold
  • Strong hook in 8–10 seconds — customer decides to keep listening within the first sentence.
  • At least 2 discovery questions — agent listens before pitching. Builds rapport and shows respect.
  • Benefit-led pitch — not "this product has X feature" but "this means you'll save X time / money."
  • Objection acknowledged, not argued — "That's a fair concern. Here's how other customers handled it…"
  • Confident assumptive close — no begging. "Shall I go ahead and set this up for you today?"
  • Clear next step regardless of outcome — sale or follow-up date. Never leave a call open-ended.

HR Topic 3 · Most Important
Initiative & Strategic Thinking
HR explicitly said: don't be basic. Show your own ideas. Be proactive, not a follower of existing process.
🚫Never say this: "I learned it from my previous TL" or "I follow what was already in place." HR will flag you as passive immediately. Every initiative must be framed as your own idea, your own system, your own observation.
📝Initiative 1: Script Improvement System
  • What I do: Every week, I listen to 10–15 recordings and tag them — strong open, weak objection, good close.
  • Pattern finding: I find the 2–3 moments appearing most in lost sales. That's where I rewrite the script.
  • A/B testing: Split team — half old script, half new. Run for one week. Compare conversion. Roll out the winner.
  • Result framing: "This system helped me improve team conversion by approximately X% within [timeframe]."
💬Say in interview: "I don't wait for the script to be updated from above. I build a feedback loop between call performance and script quality — it's a system I created and run independently."
⚡Initiative 2: Daily Tracking + Quick Coaching
  • The system: Live tracking sheet — each agent's calls, conversions, revenue updated every 2 hours.
  • Early warning: By 10AM I can see who's behind. I pull that agent aside for a 5-minute targeted fix session.
  • Why it works: A 5-minute intervention at 10AM prevents a full-day slump. EOD reviews are too late to recover.
  • Visibility effect: When the team can see each other's real-time numbers, they push themselves.
💬Say in interview: "I built this tracking system because end-of-day reviews were too late to change outcomes. Real-time visibility changes behavior."
🏆Initiative 3: Mini Competitions & Incentives
  • Weekly sprint: "First agent to hit 15 conversions this week gets [reward]." Small prize, big motivation lift.
  • Team vs team: Split into two groups. Losing half buys coffee. Creates camaraderie AND competition.
  • Personal bests: Beat your own last week's score — inclusive format where everyone can win.
  • Track the incentives: I track which formats drive sustained improvement vs. one-day spikes. I rotate to keep it fresh.
💬Say in interview: "Incentives work best when they're visible, immediate, and varied. I rotate the format so agents don't get bored with the same structure."
📚Initiative 4: Objection Playbook
  • What it is: A living document — the top 8–10 objections customers give, with 2–3 tested responses for each.
  • How I build it: Pull the most common lost-sale reasons from call notes. Write responses. Test in real calls. Keep what works.
  • Monthly update: As product and market changes, so do objections. I review and update every month.
  • Onboarding use: New agents get it on day one — cuts ramp-up time significantly.
💬Say in interview: "I treat objections as data, not roadblocks. A playbook built from real calls turns past losses into future advantages."

HR Topic 4 · Use Real Examples
Handling Tough Situations
HR wants: Situation → Analysis → Action → Result (with % if possible). Show ownership, not excuses.
⭐STAR Reminder: Situation (10%) → Task (10%) → Action (60%) → Result (20%). Most candidates spend too long on S&T. The Action is where you're actually being evaluated.
1Scenario● HardYour team's conversion rate dropped significantly for two consecutive weeks. Walk me through exactly what you did.▼
✦ Model Answer (STAR)
Situation: "Our team's conversion rate dropped from 18% to 11% over two weeks, coinciding with the launch of a new product bundle."

Task: "As Supervisor, I needed to diagnose the cause fast and recover before the monthly target was at serious risk."

Action: "First, I didn't assume — I pulled the data broken down by agent, by call stage, and by time of day. The drop was concentrated in the objection stage, specifically around the new bundle's price point.

I then listened to 15 call recordings personally — not delegated. I heard that agents were struggling to justify the bundle price vs. the previous single product.

Within 48 hours, I rewrote the price objection response — reframing it around total value, not unit cost. I ran a 30-minute focused briefing: one specific fix, not a full training. I also created a visual comparison card agents could reference during live calls.

The following week, I did live monitoring on the 3 lowest-converting agents and gave real-time feedback after each call."

Result: "By week three, conversion recovered to 16%. By week four, we hit 19% — slightly above our original baseline. The comparison card became a permanent part of our script materials."
⚡ Key: Data first, personal involvement (not delegated), specific fast action (48 hours), measurable result. That's the combination HR wants to see.
2Scenario● HardThree agents on your team are significantly underperforming and pulling down the team's revenue target. What do you do?▼
✦ Model Answer
"I start by separating the three cases — because 'underperforming' almost never has the same root cause across people.

Step 1 — Individual diagnosis: I look at each agent's data: call volume, AHT, conversion per stage. Then I listen to 3–5 of their calls. One might have high volume but low conversion — a pitch problem. Another might have low volume — motivation or time management. A third might convert on low-value deals — a targeting problem.

Step 2 — Specific 1-on-1 coaching: I meet each separately with their individual data. Not a group lecture. I show exactly where they're losing — framed as 'here's the one thing we're fixing this week,' not a criticism session.

Step 3 — Sprint target: I give each a private weekly target that's a step up from their current number — not the full team target immediately. Small wins rebuild confidence faster than big pressure.

Step 4 — Visible leaderboard: I make progress public. Even small improvements get announced. Peer visibility motivates better than anything I can say.

Result framing: "Typically within 2–3 weeks of this approach, 1–2 of the three agents recover meaningfully. If one doesn't respond after full support, I have an honest conversation about whether this role is the right fit for them."
3Scenario● HardManagement increases your monthly revenue target by 25% with no additional headcount. How do you respond?▼
✦ Model Answer
"I don't just say yes and stress the team. And I don't push back without data. I do both — commit to the direction and present a realistic plan.

My first response: 'I understand the target. Give me 24 hours to come back with a breakdown of what's achievable and what we'd need to close the remaining gap.'

Then I build the analysis: What does 25% mean per agent per day? Where is the unused capacity — agents not yet at their call volume ceiling, conversion rates below our team best, underused follow-up calls? That's where the real headroom lives.

What I present back:
— 'We can realistically reach +15% through call volume optimization and script improvement. Here's the specific plan with a timeline.'
— 'To reach +25%, we would need [X] — either a temporary resource or reduced admin burden on agents so they can spend more time selling.'

"I commit to the maximum achievable with full transparency. Overpromising and underdelivering is worse for everyone than an honest, data-backed forecast."
⚡ Key: This shows analytical grounding + professional assertiveness + solution orientation. HR values this over blind "yes." It's the answer a real leader gives.
4Scenario● MediumA top-performing agent suddenly tells you they want to resign. What do you do?▼
✦ Model Answer
"My first move is a private, honest conversation — not to talk them out of it, but to genuinely understand why.

I ask three specific questions:
— 'What would need to change for you to want to stay?'
— 'Is this about the work itself, the team, or something outside?'
— 'Have you already made your decision, or are you still considering?'

If it's fixable — workload, recognition, growth path — I propose something concrete with a real timeline. Not 'I'll talk to management.' I tell them exactly what I'll do and when. Then I actually follow through.

If the decision is final — I respect it professionally. I ask for a clean handover and an honest exit conversation. How someone leaves affects the rest of the team.

The honest reflection: "A top performer resigning is partly a retention failure, not just a surprise event. In hindsight, there were probably signs — drop in enthusiasm, less engagement. I use that as a lesson: have career conversations proactively, every single month, not reactively when someone's already decided to leave."
⚡ Key: The self-reflection at the end shows real leadership maturity — you're not just solving this case, you're learning from it systemically.
5Scenario● MediumTeam morale is low after a difficult quarter with missed targets. How do you rebuild it?▼
✦ Model Answer
"Low morale after a tough quarter usually comes from one of three things: effort not being seen, the target feeling unfair, or no visible path forward. I address each one.

Step 1 — Acknowledge it directly: I open the next team meeting by naming the difficulty. 'Last quarter was hard. I see how hard you all worked, and I want to talk about it openly.' Skipping over it or pretending it didn't happen makes things worse.

Step 2 — Reset the narrative: I reframe — what did we actually learn? What worked even if the number didn't? This isn't toxic positivity; it's extracting real value from difficulty.

Step 3 — Build early wins: I set the first two weeks of the new quarter with slightly lower internal targets — achievable ones. Confidence is rebuilt through small consecutive wins, not one big speech.

Step 4 — Bring the energy back: A low-stakes team challenge. A mini competition with a small, fun reward. Sometimes what a team needs after a tough stretch is permission to enjoy the work again.

"The goal isn't to make the team forget the hard quarter. It's to make them believe the next one can be different — and that their Supervisor has a clear plan to get there."

1Self● EasyTell me about yourself and why you're applying for this Supervisor role.▼
✦ Model Answer
"I've spent [X] years in telesales, starting as an agent and growing into a senior position where I was informally leading and coaching teammates alongside my own performance targets. I was recognized as [Best Agent / top performer] in [period].

But what I found more rewarding than my own numbers was when I helped a teammate improve their conversion rate. Seeing their results go up from a coaching conversation I led — that felt like a bigger impact than any single sale I made.

I'm applying for this Supervisor role because I want to create that impact at scale. I've built my own systems for call analysis, daily performance tracking, and team motivation. I want to apply them with the full accountability and scope of a leadership position."
2Self● MediumWhat is your biggest weakness as a leader?▼
✦ Model Answer
"My biggest weakness is that I sometimes hold high standards that not everyone on the team is immediately ready for. I can get impatient when a process I've built isn't being adopted consistently enough.

I've managed this by being explicit about timelines and building in more early check-ins. Instead of expecting instant adoption, I now say 'by end of week two, I expect everyone to be using this approach' — and I schedule two or three touchpoints in that window to catch gaps early before they become habits.

That shift from expecting results to building them deliberately has made me a more effective coach."
⚡ Key: Real weakness + specific self-correction = credibility. Interviewers immediately see through "I work too hard" style fake weaknesses.
3Leadership● MediumDescribe your leadership style.▼
✦ Model Answer
"I lead with what I call structured autonomy — I set very clear expectations and hold people accountable to outcomes, but I give them flexibility in how they get there. I'm not a micromanager, but I'm also not hands-off.

Practically, I adapt by person and situation. For a new agent, I'm more directive — clear instructions and frequent check-ins. For an experienced agent, I step back and coach rather than direct. In a crisis — a volume spike, a system outage, a major escalation — I switch to decisive, direct mode quickly.

I also believe strongly in data-driven leadership. I don't manage by gut feeling. I build visibility into the team's numbers so my coaching decisions are based on what the data actually shows, not assumptions."
4Leadership● MediumHow do you build trust with a new team quickly?▼
✦ Model Answer
"I follow a simple principle: listen first, lead second.

In the first two weeks, I have a 1-on-1 with every agent — not a performance review, a listening session. I ask: 'What's working that I shouldn't change? What makes your job harder than it needs to be? What do you need from a Supervisor that you haven't always had?'

These questions do three things: they give me real intelligence, they signal that I value each person's experience, and they set a tone of transparency that makes the whole relationship easier from day one.

Then I do small things consistently: every commitment I make, I follow through on. Trust is built in those small moments — not in big gestures. And I'm honest about what I don't know yet. A new Supervisor who pretends to know everything loses credibility fast."
5Telesales● MediumHow would you improve a team's conversion rate that has been flat for two months?▼
✦ Model Answer
"A flat conversion rate tells me one of three things: the script is stale, the targeting is wrong, or the team's skills have plateaued. I need to find which one before acting.

Week 1: Full call audit — I listen to 20 calls across the team, map exactly where in the funnel each lost sale happened.
Week 2: Rebuild the weakest stage specifically. If it's objection handling, I run a focused drill. Not a general training — one targeted session on one specific problem.
Week 3: Script variation test — one group on the updated version, one on the original. 7 days. Compare conversion daily.
Week 4: Roll out the winner. Measure the delta.

"A focused 4-week sprint like this typically moves conversion by 3–5 percentage points. On a team of 10 agents, that can mean significant revenue recovery."
6Telesales● MediumAn agent consistently struggles with objection handling. How do you coach them?▼
✦ Model Answer
"Objection handling is usually a confidence issue disguised as a knowledge problem. Most agents know the facts — they panic when the customer pushes back.

My approach: I first identify which specific objections are causing drops — price, timing, trust, or product fit. Then I give them scripted responses, but more importantly, I drill through roleplay. I play the difficult customer. Real objections from real recordings. We go 5–10 rounds until their tone shifts from defensive to calm and confident.

The goal isn't memorizing lines — it's getting comfortable enough to think clearly under pressure. I also give them one universal phrase to buy time: 'That's a really fair point — let me make sure I give you the full picture.' Three seconds, composure maintained, leads naturally into the response."
7Initiative● HardTell me about something you created or initiated that improved your team's performance — without being asked.▼
✦ Model Answer (STAR — Your Signature Story)
Situation: "I noticed that even though we had a team script, every agent was using it slightly differently — and conversion rates varied by as much as 8 percentage points between our best and bottom agents."

Task: "Nobody asked me to fix this. But I knew the inconsistency was costing us real revenue every single day."

Action: "I spent two weeks listening to the top-converting calls from our best agents. I extracted the specific phrases, timing, and pacing that made them work. I built an updated script and a companion objection playbook — tested it informally with two agents first, refined it based on their feedback, then presented it to the full team with a one-week test run."

Result: "Within one month, the performance gap between top and bottom agents narrowed from 8 percentage points to 3. Overall team conversion improved by approximately 12%. The playbook became part of our standard onboarding for every new agent."
⚡ This is your signature initiative story. Rehearse every detail until it flows naturally and confidently — this is the answer that separates you from passive candidates.
8People● MediumHow do you balance pushing for results while keeping your team from burning out?▼
✦ Model Answer
"I think the tension between performance and wellbeing is a false choice if you manage it with intentionality. The key is sustainable intensity — not pushing at 100% every day, but building a rhythm with peaks and deliberate recovery.

Practically: I push hard during campaign and target periods, but I build lighter activities after intense stretches. I watch for burnout signals — drop in call quality, shorter calls, quieter presence in team chat, less initiative. When I see them, I address it in a 1-on-1 immediately, before it becomes a resignation.

I also believe that when agents understand why a target matters — how it connects directly to their own earnings and career — they self-motivate. Pressure from understanding is sustainable. Pressure from fear is not. My job is to create the first kind."
9People● HardTwo agents have a conflict that's affecting the whole team's dynamic. What do you do?▼
✦ Model Answer
"The worst moves here are to ignore it or handle it publicly. Both destroy trust for different reasons.

Step 1: I meet each agent separately. Not to judge — to understand. I listen fully. I ask: 'What happened from your perspective?' and 'What do you need to move forward?'

Step 2: I let both cool down. Then I bring them together privately. Before we start, I set one rule: 'We're not here to decide who was right. We're here to agree on how we work together from today.'

Step 3: I facilitate — not judge. I summarize each person's position and ask them each to commit to one specific behavioral change going forward.

Step 4: I monitor for the next two weeks. Conflict that seems resolved but isn't followed up will resurface.

"On the floor, professional respect is non-negotiable. Friendship is optional. I make that expectation clear from the start, which prevents most conflicts from escalating in the first place."
10Self● EasyWhere do you see yourself in 2–3 years?▼
✦ Model Answer
"In 2–3 years, I want to have built a telesales team that consistently performs in the top tier — not because they're pressured to, but because they genuinely understand the impact of their work and take pride in it.

I also want to have developed at least one agent from my team who's ready for a Supervisor role themselves. That's how I measure real leadership success — not by my own advancement, but by how many people advanced because of the environment I created.

Beyond the team level, I'm interested in contributing at a program level — improving how we onboard new agents, how we structure scripts for new product launches, how we build the systems that make the whole operation more consistent."

Delivery · English · Presence
Tips & Power Phrases
How to deliver answers with confidence, clarity, and strategic framing — in full English, every time.
🎙️Full English practice drill: Record yourself answering each section out loud on your phone. Listen back. The goal is that when you're nervous on interview day, your English flows from muscle memory — not from translating in your head or searching for words.
✅Do This
  • Lead with the main point first. Don't build up slowly — state the conclusion, then explain. "The first thing I do is pull the data. Here's why..."
  • Use numbers whenever possible. "Conversion improved by 12%" is 10x more credible than "conversion got better."
  • Pause before hard questions. "That's a great question — give me a moment to think." Sounds confident, not lost.
  • Own your initiatives. Use "I built," "I created," "I designed" — never "we used to do" or "my TL showed me."
  • Frame all problems as solved. Not "this was very difficult" but "here's what I did, and here's what happened."
  • Ask two smart questions at the end. Prepared questions signal genuine interest and preparation.
❌Avoid This
  • Switching to Bahasa — even one word. Stay in English for the entire interview, even under pressure.
  • "I learned this from my TL." HR explicitly flagged this. Every idea you share must be framed as yours.
  • Answers over 2 minutes. After 120 seconds, most interviewers have stopped listening. Practice timing yourself.
  • General statements with no examples. "I'm good at coaching" is worthless. Show a specific moment and result.
  • Blaming external factors. Always show what YOU did in response — not what happened to you.
  • "I don't know" without a follow-up. Always add: "but here's how I'd find out and what I'd do."
💬Power Phrases — Memorise These, Use Them Naturally
I don't wait to be told there's a problem. I build systems that surface the problem before it becomes critical.
My job as a Supervisor isn't to be the best salesperson on the team. It's to create the environment where every agent on the team can be their best.
I treat objections as data. Every time a customer says no, they're telling me exactly where the script needs to improve.
When performance drops, I go to the data first and assumptions last. The data tells a more accurate story than gut feeling.
Motivation through fear is short-term. Motivation through visible progress — when people can see their own momentum — is sustainable.
A team that needs me present to perform hasn't been built properly. My goal is to create independence, not dependency on me.
I can teach skills. What I can't teach is the willingness to be coached. That's why attitude is the first thing I look for.
🎤Smart Questions to Ask the Interviewer
  • "What does success look like for this Supervisor role in the first 90 days?"
  • "What is the team's biggest performance challenge right now — and what's been tried so far?"
  • "How much autonomy does a Supervisor have to implement their own systems — for example, script changes or incentive structures?"
  • "What is the current team size, and what is the typical agent tenure?"
  • "How does the Supervisor role interact with the product and campaign teams at TikTok Shop?"
💡Why this matters: Candidates who ask specific, intelligent questions are seen as more serious, more prepared, and more likely to succeed. Asking nothing signals a lack of genuine interest.
